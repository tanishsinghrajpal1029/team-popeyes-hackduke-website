01 / THE MISSION

Share the excitement equally.

A qubit is a quantum version of a bit. Here, an excitation simply means a 1. A Dicke state shares exactly n ones across N qubits in every possible position.

For four qubits and two 1s, there are six valid answers. We want all six to have equal quantum amplitude and the right relative phase.

Why not simply choose the shortest path? A SWAP typically costs several entangling gates. One bad coupler can erase the gain from a shorter route. Our actual submission weighs calibrated gate error, readout error, idle time, and expected score when choosing a placement.

HOW THE ALTERNATIVE PATH IS FOUND

Turn the chip into a weighted map.

Each qubit is a node and each coupler is an edge. Marking a qubit unavailable removes it from both searches. The gray route minimizes hop count. The green route runs Dijkstra’s algorithm again, this time minimizing estimated gate error.

  1. Price each move. If a coupler has illustrative CZ error p, a preterminal SWAP costs −3 ln(1−p); the final interaction costs −ln(1−p).
  2. Choose the smallest total. Adding those costs is equivalent to maximizing ∏(1−p)m, where m is 3 for a SWAP or 1 for the final interaction.
  3. Compare and explain. The path readout above lists the exact qubits, SWAP count, and estimated gate survival for both choices. A longer detour wins when its lower edge errors offset the extra moves.

03 / THE TWO CHECKS

The same answers can hide a different state.

The challenge checks measurements in both Z and X bases. Z asks whether the excitation count and probabilities look right. X probes the phases that make it a coherent quantum superposition.
